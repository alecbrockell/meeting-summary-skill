# Meeting summary

Produce an in-depth thematic account of the meeting, not minutes and not an action list.

## Lead with the outcome

Open with the central outcome, change in thinking, key decision, or most important unresolved issue. If the discussion reached no decision, say so and identify the live alternatives without manufacturing consensus.

## Organize by theme

- Group related discussion under short, substantive headings when they improve navigation
- Explain the reasoning and evidence that matter to each theme
- Distinguish decisions from proposals, participant claims, hypotheses, limitations, and open questions
- Preserve material disagreements, counterinterpretations, and uncertainty
- Name a speaker only when attribution changes the meaning, such as a personal view, assignment, or disagreement
- Translate jargon for an intelligent reader unfamiliar with the meeting when necessary

Use enough detail to preserve the logic and decision boundary, but omit small talk, repetition, scheduling chatter, and procedural residue. Do not follow transcript order merely because it is available.

## Keep actions separate

Do not turn the summary into assignments or repeat the action list in prose. When action items are also requested, end the summary cleanly and place execution records in a separate `Action items` section governed by [action-items.md](action-items.md).

Use only the supplied record and specifically supplied context unless the user expressly asks for outside research.
